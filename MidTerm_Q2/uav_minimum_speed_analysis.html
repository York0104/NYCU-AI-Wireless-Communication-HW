Derivation from the textbook formula

Disc loading: v_i = k_n·(p / 2ρ)^(1/2) [Eq. 3.7 in book] k_n = 1.05–1.20 (correction factor, typically 1.1) p = disc loading [N/m²] ρ = 1.225 kg/m³ (sea level air density) For fixed-wing stall speed (equivalent): V_min = √(2·(W/S) / (ρ·CL_max)) Fitted CL_max ≈ 1.0 from Fig. 3.4 calibration: At W/S = 50 N/m² → V_min = √(100/1.225) = 9.04 m/s ✓ matches graph At W/S = 25 N/m² → V_min = √(50/1.225) = 6.39 m/s ✓ matches graph At W/S = 10 N/m² → V_min = √(20/1.225) = 4.04 m/s ✓ matches graph → ABSOLUTE MINIMUM on graph: approaches 0 as W/S → 0 → PRACTICAL MAV minimum: ~3.2 m/s at W/S ≈ 5 N/m² → PRACTICAL Mini UAV minimum: ~6.4 m/s at W/S ≈ 25 N/m²

What "minimum speed" means for a rotary-wing UAV

Rotary-wing (quadcopter) minimum airspeed: V_min_airspeed = 0 m/s ← can hover indefinitely BUT: propellers must spin to generate lift. Blade tip speed: V_tip = π × Dp × fm = π × 0.254 × 77 = 61.4 m/s (propeller tip always moves at 61.4 m/s even when hovering) Reflection zone angle (Eq. 4 in paper): θrz = sin⁻¹(Dp / 4·dant) = sin⁻¹(0.254 / (4×0.200)) = sin⁻¹(0.3175) ≈ 0.32 rad Maximum micro-Doppler frequency (Eq. 5, at θe = θrz): fD,r_max = 4π·fm·dant·fc·sin(2θrz) / c = 4π × 77 × 0.200 × 2.5×10⁹ × sin(0.64) / (3×10⁸) ≈ 963.09 Hz ✓ confirmed in paper Reflection period (how often a blade sweeps past): T_reflect = 60 / (fm × Nb) = 60 / (4620 × 2) ≈ 6.49 ms
Key result: the UAV can fly at 0 m/s (hover), but its propellers create a micro-Doppler shift of up to 963 Hz — a channel impairment that exists even during stationary hovering and must be compensated.

How both papers connect to your FLAME paper

Textbook Figure 3.4 → FLAME fixed-wing extension FLAME currently assumes rotary-wing (hovering). If you extend to fixed-wing follower UAVs (e.g., fixed-wing scouts covering larger areas), the minimum speed constraint V_min = √(2W/S / ρCL_max) becomes an additional trajectory constraint in the optimization. Fixed-wing cannot stop to hover during FL rounds — they must orbit, adding delay.
Hou et al. → FLAME channel model upgrade FLAME uses a static channel gain g_i. The Hou et al. paper shows that even a hovering rotary-wing UAV introduces 963 Hz micro-Doppler from propeller rotation. This means g_i is not static but time-varying with period ≈6.5ms. For NOMA-based FL (your paper mentions NOMA), this is especially damaging as micro-Doppler distorts SIC ordering.
Pman(L) + hover physics connection From Abeywickrama et al. (the empirical paper), hovering power P_hover = 236.62W baseline. From the textbook Eq.(3.7): v_i = k_n(p/2ρ)^0.5 is the induced velocity. The induced power P_i = k_n·T·v_i directly explains why Pman is the dominant energy term in FLAME — rotary-wing hover power is always "on" during FL training.
Minimum speed → communication model implication Fixed-wing: must maintain V ≥ V_min ≈ 6–15 m/s → always a translation Doppler f_D = V·fc/c ≈ 6×2.5GHz/3×10⁸ = 50 Hz. Rotary-wing: V_airspeed = 0 but f_micro-Doppler = 963 Hz from propellers. The rotary-wing micro-Doppler (963 Hz) is 19× larger than typical translation Doppler for mini UAVs — justifying why Hou et al. argue it cannot be ignored.

Citation text for your paper

"Rotary-wing UAVs can hover at zero airspeed, making them suitable for stationary FL data collection. However, even during hover, propeller rotation at f_m = 4620 RPM introduces micro-Doppler frequency shifts up to 963 Hz in sub-6 GHz channels [Hou et al., IEEE WCL 2021], causing time-varying channel conditions that our current static channel model g_i does not capture. Addressing this dynamic channel effect is left as future work."
